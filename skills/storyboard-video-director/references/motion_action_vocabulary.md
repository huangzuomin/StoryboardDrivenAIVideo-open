# Motion And Action Vocabulary

Use this file when Beats feel too static or when the user asks for chase, dance, disaster, fight, product transformation, or any high-motion storyboard.

Sources distilled: StudioBinder storyboard arrows/action storyboarding, animation-principles material, slapstick timing research abstracts, stage combat guidance, Laban Movement Analysis references, rescue terminology, and product visualization references.

## Core Action Rules

1. Board action as readable beats: anticipation, launch, contact, recoil, recovery, new intention.
2. Use verbs with force and direction: `lunges frame-left`, `ricochets backward`, `slides under`, `snaps upright`.
3. Separate body/object motion from camera motion.
4. Add momentum: `carried by speed`, `overcorrects`, `skids past target`, `weight drops into heels`.
5. Make impact visible through deformation, reaction, sound cue, debris, or follow-through.
6. For comedy, stage setup, pause, reversal, payoff, and final tag.
7. For fights, prioritize intention, contact illusion, reaction, and readability.
8. For dance, describe effort quality, not only pose.

## Prompt-Ready Beat Formula

```text
[shot size/camera] + [subject] + [anticipation] + [main verb with direction] + [momentum/impact] + [reaction/follow-through] + [timing note]
```

Example:

```text
Wide shot, handheld chase energy: the cat glances back, bolts frame-right, skids across loose papers, shoulder-bumps a table into a cascading spill, then freezes for a one-beat comic realization.
```

## Acceleration / Chase

Verbs:

- bolts, darts, sprints, barrels, rockets, launches, surges, bounds, charges, tears away
- skids, fishtails, overcorrects, careens, swerves, cuts across, doubles back, vaults over
- slips through, squeezes past, threads between, ducks under, scrambles up, tumbles down
- gains ground, loses footing, recovers stride, glances back, commits forward

Use with:

- left-to-right screen direction
- obstacle geography
- near-miss spacing
- reaction shots
- debris or prop consequence

Prompt phrases:

```text
The mouse darts frame-right under chair legs while the cat's body momentum carries it too far; the camera whip-pans to catch the overcorrection.
```

```text
The chase line zigzags through the room, with each panel escalating the mess: papers scatter, lamp tilts, curtain tears, shelf buckles.
```

## Impact / Collision

Verbs:

- slams, thuds, smacks, clips, bumps, crashes, collides, bounces, rebounds, buckles
- absorbs, crumples, compresses, jars, jolts, snaps back, recoils, shakes off
- splinters, scatters, bursts, sprays, rattles, tips, topples, rolls, clatters

Prompt phrases:

```text
On impact, the body compresses into a squash pose, cheeks and fur lagging behind, then rebounds with loose follow-through.
```

```text
The chair leg clips the table; objects fan outward in a clean arc, with a half-beat pause before the final cup drops.
```

## Physical Comedy Timing

Terms:

- double-take, freeze, slow realization, delayed reaction, blink-beat, held pose
- near-miss, false recovery, overcorrection, delayed collapse, dignity beat, final tag
- setup, plant, escalation, reversal, payoff, topper

Rules:

- Let the setup read before the payoff.
- Keep physical danger harmless and readable.
- Use a final small reaction after the big event.

Prompt phrases:

```text
Hold the cat in a proud almost-victory pose for one beat; then the shelf collapses behind it in a delayed payoff.
```

```text
The mouse freezes in a tiny smug pose after escaping; the cat notices too late and gives a slow embarrassed blink.
```

## Contemporary Dance / Performance

Laban effort actions:

- `press`: direct, sustained, heavy, bound
- `flick`: indirect, quick, light, free
- `wring`: indirect, sustained, heavy, bound
- `dab`: direct, quick, light, bound
- `slash`: indirect, quick, heavy, free
- `glide`: direct, sustained, light, free
- `punch`: direct, quick, heavy, bound
- `float`: indirect, sustained, light, free

Choreography terms:

- suspend, release, contract, spiral, melt, fold, unfold, tilt, hinge, sweep
- counterbalance, ripple, isolate, rebound, carve, reach, collapse, recover
- weight shift, floorwork, lift, fall, suspension, breath phrase, hair whip

Prompt phrases:

```text
The dancer glides forward with sustained lightness, spirals through the torso, then drops into a heavy bound press against the wet floor.
```

```text
Full-body side silhouette: she gathers weight into bent knees, punches upward through the arms, suspends at full reach, then melts into floorwork.
```

## Fight / Action

Verbs:

- squares up, feints, parries, checks, blocks, slips, ducks, weaves, pivots
- drives forward, closes distance, breaks line, redirects force, sweeps, hooks, shoves
- staggers, drops guard, absorbs the hit, sells the blow, recoils, resets stance
- disarms, pins, grapples, clinches, wrenches free, rolls out, scrambles back

Rules:

- Keep intention clear before contact.
- Show contact illusion and reaction, not random violence.
- Preserve eye lines, stance, and screen direction.

## Disaster Rescue

Terms:

- search, locate, stabilize, extricate, triage, evacuate, shore, brace, breach
- assess collapse zone, clear debris, enter void, mark hazard, establish cordon
- lift, drag, carry, package patient, transfer to stretcher, monitor secondary collapse

Rules:

- Keep movement procedural and respectful.
- Alternate chaos bursts with decision beats.
- Show hazard geography before rescue action.

## Product Transformation

Verbs:

- rotate, orbit, turntable, unfold, bloom, separate, fan out, telescope, slide open
- explode outward, align on axis, hover in layers, snap together, self-assemble
- peel away, cross-section, cutaway, reveal internals, isolate component, label callout
- morph, dissolve, wipe, push, parallax push, zoom reveal, materialize

Rules:

- Preserve spatial relationships and assembly order.
- Use hero angle, macro detail, exploded view, and packshot as separate Beats.

## Sport Performance / Rhythm Technique

Use when the subject is a technical physical performance where rhythm, skill escalation, and repeated graphic paths are the product.

General performance verbs:

- plants, springs, rebounds, suspends, snaps down, pivots, threads, crosses, uncrosses, whips, slices, carves, locks, releases
- accelerates, syncopates, doubles, triples, catches the beat, breaks the beat, lands clean, resets, freezes, holds
- orbits, spirals, loops, traces, rings, slashes, flicks, stamps, skims, taps, skates, glides, vaults

Jump rope vocabulary:

- ready stance, side swing, basic bounce, boxer step, alternating step, high-knee step, heel-toe switch
- double-under, triple-under, cross, side-cross, figure-eight, criss-cross release, mic release, leg-over, rope wrap, speed step
- rope arc, ghost rope trail, beat circle, infinity loop, floor strike, wrist snap, ankle skim, air pause, rope catch, controlled stop

Jump rope escalation example:

```text
ready stance -> first side swing -> fast alternating steps -> double-under -> cross and figure-eight -> close foot percussion -> orbiting flow state -> suspended air pause -> triple-under combo -> fisheye lean into beat -> speed ramp -> ground-break impact -> hero build -> ultra-wide 360 energy ring -> final controlled stop
```

Parkour vocabulary:

- run-up, precision jump, wall run, tic-tac, cat leap, vault, kong vault, lazy vault, underbar, rail balance, roll-out, rooftop gap, wall pop, drop landing

Skating vocabulary:

- push-off, carve, powerslide, ollie, kickflip, grind, spin entry, landing lock, edge change, spray arc, speed line, low crouch, rebound turn

Gymnastics / acrobatic vocabulary:

- hurdle step, roundoff, back handspring, aerial, layout, twist, tuck, split leap, ring leap, handstand press, controlled stick, rebound pass

Boxing / footwork vocabulary:

- bounce rhythm, jab step, slip line, pivot, check hook, shuffle out, angle change, rope drill, heavy bag impact, shoulder roll, reset guard

Technique escalation rules:

- Begin with a simple readable base action.
- Introduce one signature technique by panel 3 or 4.
- Add a variation that changes path, level, or body orientation.
- Include one close insert where the rhythm becomes tactile.
- Include one suspension or air-pause Beat before peak speed.
- Peak with a combo, speed ramp, or full-body risk moment.
- Resolve with a controlled stop or held identity pose.

Prompt phrases:

```text
The rope starts as a simple red side arc, becomes a perfect overhead beat circle, mutates into a figure-eight cross, then expands into a full-frame energy ring before resolving as a clean curve on the floor.
```

```text
Close insert: the sneaker stamps the beat as the rope kisses the concrete, dust pops outward in a small percussion burst, then the body rebounds upward into the next trick.
```

## Beat Quality Checklist

- Does the Beat name direction?
- Does it include weight or momentum?
- Does it show a consequence?
- Does it define camera movement separately?
- Does it avoid the generic verbs `moves`, `goes`, `walks`, and `looks` when a stronger verb fits?
- Does it contain an anticipation or reaction when action readability matters?
